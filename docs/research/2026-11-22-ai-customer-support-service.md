# AI + human customer support for online stores: is there demand, and should Thabiso build it?

Checked: 2026-11-22
Scope: a separate business idea from the Totallydifferent store. The store matters here only as a free demo.

## Bottom line

1. Yes, people are paying for this, but they mostly buy the two halves separately: cheap AI tools at about $0.75-$1.25 per resolved conversation, and human outsourced agents at about $8-$35 per hour or $1,000-$1,400 per agent per month. Very few services sell AI and human help together at a price a small store can afford.
2. The best-known service that did sell both (Simplr, about $2.25 per resolution) appears to have shut down in late 2026 (unverified, single competitor source). That leaves a real but narrow gap: a monthly package for stores with fewer than about 500 tickets a month that gives them AI answers plus a real person when the bot gets stuck.
3. Setting up a chatbot on its own is not a business. Fiverr gigs sell it for about $50-$400. The money is in running support every month, where his call-centre experience counts.
4. Recommendation: do not build an AI model. Run a 30-day paid pilot with 2-3 small stores. Use an off-the-shelf AI agent on the store's own helpdesk account and do the human escalations himself. Use Totallydifferent only as the free demo.

## Where the gap is

- **The software is cheap and everywhere.** No one needs Thabiso to build an AI.
- **Human BPOs sell by the seat.** $1,000-$1,400 per agent per month (Influx) or $11+/hour (Hugo) is too much for a store with 100-500 tickets a month.
- **The service that mixed AI and humans per resolution (Simplr) seems to be gone.** PartnerHero's managed AI is $1.25 per solve, but its human side is sold only through sales calls.
- **The opening:** a small store that wants an AI set up for it, someone to check and fix the AI's answers every week, and a trained human for refunds, angry customers and "where is my order" exceptions. All for one monthly price, with no seat contract. His call-centre experience (quality checks, escalation scripts, tone) is the part Fiverr setup sellers and the AI tools do not offer.
- **Honest caveat:** the gap is narrow. Helpdesk vendors are adding human handoff, and $3-6/hour Philippine VAs already pair human support with AI tools ([OnlineJobs.ph](https://www.onlinejobs.ph/jobseekers/job/1614247)).

## Risks

1. **The market is commoditising.** Bot setup costs $50-$400 on Fiverr, and AI tools keep getting cheaper. Sell the monthly managed service, not the setup.
2. **The AI says something wrong**, such as promising a refund or giving the wrong delivery date. The contract must say the store's policies are the source of truth and limit his liability. **Not legally checked.**
3. **Data protection.**
   - As a South African business, he falls under POPIA.
   - Section 72 limits sending personal data out of SA unless a condition is met. A data transfer agreement is the usual route ([CMS](https://cms.law/en/zaf/legal-updates/Managing-cross-border-data-transfers), [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/southafrica/privacy-protection/973962/international-data-transfers-what-are-the-adequate-levels-of-protection-required-by-popia)).
   - For EU/UK stores he would act as a "processor" under GDPR, which normally needs a written processor agreement (Art. 28) and an approved transfer mechanism, such as standard contractual clauses, for data reaching SA. **Unverified in this session. Confirm with a privacy lawyer before signing an EU/UK client.**
   - Practical safeguard: work inside the client's own helpdesk account (Gorgias, Zendesk and so on) so customer data never sits on his systems.
4. **Company registration.**
   - A sole proprietor needs no company registration. A (Pty) Ltd must register with CIPC and file annual returns ([Xero ZA](https://www.xero.com/za/resources/accounting-glossary/s/what-is-sole-proprietorship)).
   - SA tax residents pay tax on worldwide income, so foreign client fees must be declared, and he will likely be a provisional taxpayer ([TaxTim](https://app.taxtim.com/answers/how-do-i-pay-tax-on-my-foreign-earned-income), [Xero provisional tax](https://www.xero.com/za/guides/provisional-tax-south-africa/)).
   - Whether overseas clients will contract with a sole proprietor, and how exchange control treats USD receipts: **unverified, ask a tax practitioner.**
5. **One-person capacity and time zones.** SA (UTC+2) covers UK/EU daytime well. US evenings would need a second person or AI-only cover.
6. **Low-cost rivals.** Philippine VAs at $3-6/hour undercut SA wages. Compete on quality, English and contact-centre management, not price.

## Recommended next step: a small paid pilot

1. **Demo (free):** put an off-the-shelf AI agent on Totallydifferent's chat once the store is live.
   - Feed it the store's rules: free delivery from R750, otherwise R80; ZAR only; Paystack.
   - Order lookup can use the existing `/api/orders/:ref` endpoint, through a builder spec later, not now.
   - Record a 2-minute screen video of the bot answering and handing off to him.
2. **Pilot (paid, 30 days):** offer 2-3 small English-speaking Shopify stores a fixed fee, for example $299-$599/month.
   - The store keeps its own helpdesk and AI tool account and pays those fees itself.
   - He provides setup, weekly answer tuning, and human escalations during agreed hours.
   - No AI model building and no bootcamp. Use Gorgias, Tidio, Help Scout or Fin as the client prefers.
3. **Pass rule, fixed before starting:**
   - At least 2 of the first 20 stores approached agree to pay.
   - The AI resolves at least 50% of conversations.
   - Human time stays under 15 hours per store per month.
   - If any of these fails, stop or rethink.
4. **Before the first paid client:** a one-page service agreement with a data processing clause, checked by a lawyer. Confirm with a tax practitioner how he will invoice and receive USD.

This agent has not contacted anyone or spent money. The owner does all outreach.
