3. **Skip for now:** AZ Hair Vietnam (3.1 on 4 reviews, two 1-stars allege synthetic blend) and Lyn Hair (1 review). Anbi, Govihair, Unihairvn, Duc Manh, Minh Khang, Foxen and Nanming: I found no independent evidence either way, so they are "unverified", not "bad".
4. **Margin:** if the Alibaba unit prices fall in the US$49-129 band, landed cost is roughly R1,300-R3,150 per piece. That is a 40-55% margin at the SA stock prices you gave, before payment fees and returns. Alibaba prices at that level are almost certainly **not true Vietnamese raw hair**; see the authenticity section.
5. **Duty:** SA general duty on HS 6704.20 (human-hair wigs) is 20%, plus 15% VAT. China gets no preference. Confirm the tariff code with a clearing agent.

## 2. Landed cost in ZAR (illustrative)

**Alibaba unit prices below are placeholders inside the only price range I saw (Megalook US$49-129 for a 5x5 HD closure wig). They are not quotes. Replace them with real quotes.**

Assumptions:
- **FX:** US$1 = R16.76 (OFX mid-market, 13 Nov 2026, [OFX](https://www.ofx.com/en-us/exchange-rates/zar)). Other sources that week ranged R16.37-16.93. Bank/card rates are worse.
- **Freight:** express courier at about US$10/kg (guides quote US$7.5-14.5/kg, [Tonlexing 2026](https://www.tonlexing.com/shipping-cost-from-china-to-south-africa/)), 0.5 kg packed per wig, so about US$5 per piece. Assumes you consolidate about 10 pieces per parcel. These are forwarder blogs; I found no DHL quote.
- **Duty:** 20% on goods + freight, assuming HS 6704.20 ("articles of human hair"). Source: [JLOG SA tariff page](https://jlog.co.za/hs-code/hs-6704-20-wigs-false-beards-eyebrows-and-eyelashes-switches-and-the-like-of-human-or-animal-hair-or-of-textile-materials-articles-of-human-hair-not-elsewhere-specified-or-included-of/), "last verified 15 Nov 2026 from SARS tariff book". SADC/EU/UK are free; AfCFTA 8%. China and Vietnam have no preference. Loose hair/closures may classify differently (cf. SARS v Fascination Wigs, [SCA 2010](https://www1.saflii.org/za/cases/ZASCA/2010/6.html)). Confirm with a clearing agent.
- **VAT:** 15% on (goods + freight + duty). In practice SARS also adds a 10% uplift to the VAT base; I did not verify that here, so real VAT may be about R20-R40 higher per piece.
- **Clearing/courier handling:** R400 per parcel, split over 10 pieces = R40/piece. This is my assumption, unverified.
- **Not included:** Paxi delivery to customers, Paystack fees, returns, ad spend.

## 3. Authenticity risk

- Alibaba's "Verified" badge and "Trade Assurance" show company registration and payment protection. They do not prove hair origin or that the hair is human.
- Price signal: Alibaba's own buyer guide puts raw virgin hair at US$180-350/kg and non-remy at US$30-80/kg ([Alibaba seller blog](https://seller.alibaba.com/blogs/2026/southeast-asia/beauty/human-hair-wigs-b2b-buying-guide-alibaba), 2026-11-20). A 220g HD lace wig at US$49-129 implies processed or non-single-donor hair. Do not market it as "Vietnamese raw".
- Same blog says Xuchang makes about 60% of the world's wigs (vendor-affiliated, treat as indicative).
- The Vietnamese leads are not safe either: Trustpilot shows synthetic-blend and wrong-lace-shade complaints for AZ Hair and Kfhair.

Ask every supplier for:
1. Factory address, business licence, and a live video call walking the floor.
2. Written hair origin claim. Customs documents (certificate of origin) for Vietnam claims.
3. Photos and video of your actual sample, not stock images: cuticle alignment, lace shade (HD vs transparent), density, cap size.
4. Per-piece weight (220g/300g) confirmed on a scale.
5. Refund and return terms in writing, and payment through Trade Assurance only.

On arrival, test: burn test (human hair burns slowly to ash with a burnt-hair smell, synthetic melts into a bead), 100-stroke brush shed test, 3 washes for colour and tangling, 3-day wear.

Third-party inspection (SGS, BV) costs US$200-400 per visit; only worth it above the R20-25k plan.

## 4. Legal risk

- **Consumer Protection Act:** labelling something "Vietnamese raw human hair" when it is processed or Chinese-origin is a misrepresentation risk. Describe by what you can prove. Keep supplier proof and test photos.
- **Counterfeit/IP:** avoid branded cap packaging or logos you do not own. Order unbranded or your own label.
- **POPIA:** applies to your customers' data, not to supplier sourcing.
- **Customs:** misclassification or under-declaration gets goods detained. Have the supplier invoice show real value.
